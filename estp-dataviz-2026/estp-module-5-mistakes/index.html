What is wrong with this chart?

The values did not change. The visual impression did.

A technically correct scale can still make variation look larger than the evidence warrants. The question is not whether the axis starts at zero. It is whether the framing supports a proportionate reading.

Course example · SSB table 08518

Unemployment rate appears to swing dramatically

Both sexes · age 15–74 · quarterly · per cent of the labour force

Inspect the chart before revealing the design problem.

The data never changes.

The plotting range is only one percentage point wide.

The 4.1–5.0% range therefore occupies almost the full height of the chart. In the fixed version the axis still does not start at zero; it simply gives the variation more visual context.

Takeaway 1 · Scale

A chart can be technically correct and still be misleading.

The scale must support a proportionate interpretation. The numbers may be right while the visual impression is exaggerated.

Correct numbers are not enough. The visual framing must also support a correct interpretation.

Five common pitfalls · 2

More information can make the relevant pattern harder to see.

The question here is simple: how did overall unemployment move? Adding every available age series and every value is accurate, but it gives the reader several competing tasks.

Course example · SSB table 08518

Unemployment by age · 2025Q1–2026Q2

Four legitimate series, equal emphasis and 24 value labels compete for attention.

Nothing false is shown. The problem is that the chart makes the reader solve several questions that are not needed for the stated task.

Takeaway 2 · Clutter

Too much information is also a form of distortion.

Showing everything is not automatically more neutral. Remove only what does not help answer the question.

Five common pitfalls · 3

Context is part of accuracy.

A short window can turn a local movement into the whole story. A longer window is not automatically better; it is better when the claim requires it.

Takeaway 3 · Context

The time window should match the claim being made.

Baselines, denominators, time frames and comparison groups can determine whether a correct number is interpreted correctly.

Takeaway 4 · Emphasis

Visual prominence should correspond to analytical importance.

Strong colour, area and contrast are arguments about what matters. Use them only when the evidence and question support that argument.

Five common pitfalls · 5

A precise-looking chart should not imply more certainty than the statistics provide.

The Labour Force Survey is sample-based. Table 08518 does not provide a confidence interval in this teaching extract, so the page does not invent one.

SSB Labour Force Survey

Quarterly unemployment rate · 2025Q1–2026Q2

The chart looks exact because only point estimates are visible.

Sample-based estimate

Sampling error exists because the LFS surveys a sample rather than the entire population.

Quarterly estimates vary more

SSB states that standard errors are substantially lower for annual averages than for quarterly averages.

Do not over-read adjacent points

SSB advises caution when assessing changes over time from one survey to another.

2021Q1 · major LFS redesignSSB estimates a break of about 5,400 unemployed persons. That break estimate was not statistically significant; the new unemployment rate was estimated 0.1 percentage points higher than the old LFS.

Takeaway 5 · Uncertainty

Uncertainty should not disappear in the visualization.

Estimates, revisions and methodological breaks belong in the interpretation when they can change what the reader concludes.
