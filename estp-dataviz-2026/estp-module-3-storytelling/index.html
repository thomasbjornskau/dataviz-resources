Turn data into a visual story

A chart shows data. A visual story helps the reader understand what matters in it.

data→observation→focus→context→narrative

Storytelling is the deliberate sequencing of attention.

From chart to story

The data stays the same. The storytelling layer changes.

Context, not a causal claim

First births are happening later

Mean age of mothers at first birth rose from 23.3 years in 1968 to 30.4 in 2025. This provides useful context about the timing of parenthood.

The two developments occurred over the same broad period. This chart alone does not establish that one caused the other.

The story, stated carefully

Norway’s fertility rate has moved through distinct phases: a steep fall in the 1970s, a partial recovery that culminated in 2009, then a long decline to a record low in 2023 followed by a modest rise in 2024–2025.

Step 1 of 6
Total fertility rate is an indicator, not a headcount.

SSB defines it as the average number of live-born children a woman would have if the age-specific fertility rates observed in that year applied throughout ages 15–49, assuming no mortality. It is a period measure, not the completed number of children actually born to a cohort of women.

Start with a real statistical insight. Then make it easier to see — without making the data say more than it actually does.

2 · Guide attention deliberately

Hierarchy changes the reading experience, not the data

A chart does not need to make every element equally prominent. The author can decide what should be read first while keeping the full statistical content available.

Everything competes. Gridlines, labels and points all ask for attention.

Where did you look first?

Annotations should add meaning

Label the relevance, not just the value

Weak annotation

2025Fertility rate, 2025: 1.48

It repeats information the axis and point can already provide.

Useful annotation

2025After the 2023 low of 1.40, TFR rose to 1.48 in 2025 — a change in direction, not a return to earlier levels.

It tells the reader why the point matters to the argument.

Useful annotations can mark a turning point, unusual value, change in direction, sourced event or meaningful comparison. Keep them close to the data they explain.

There is no universal answer. A table might belong in an appendix; a comparison might be redundant if the previous chart already makes it obvious. Edit for this narrative, not for data completeness.

5 · Do not let storytelling override statistical integrity

When the story becomes too strong

A true dataset can support a distorted impression with surprisingly little manipulation: narrow the time window, highlight only one movement, or write a headline that claims more than the evidence.

Headline

Fertility rose in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low

The longer series still shows a much lower level than in 2009 or the late 1960s.

Time windowAnnotationHeadline

Context versus causality

Evidence: Mean age at first birth increased while fertility declined over much of the period after 2010. The two series alone do not establish why.

Module 3

Good storytelling does not add drama to the data. It removes the work required to see what matters.

1Start with a genuine statistical message, not a pile of charts. 2Guide attention deliberately with hierarchy, contrast, annotation and sequence. 3Make every chart earn its place by contributing new evidence. 4Use context and comparison to make the statistical meaning easier to see. 5Protect statistical integrity: do not cherry-pick evidence or turn correlation into causation.
