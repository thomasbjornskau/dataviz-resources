Audience changes the frame

The same data can require different visualizations for different audiences

A policy analyst, journalist and general reader may need different levels of detail, context and explanation. The statistics can remain identical while selection, framing, annotations and visual complexity change.

Takeaway 3 · conceptual centre

Context gives meaning to numbers

Start with one correct number. Then add context deliberately. The question is not “How much can we add?” but “Which comparison changes interpretation?”

Consumer prices increased3.0%over the last 12 months
Does the number mean the same thing now?

Alone, 3.0% answers “how much?” but gives little help with “is that high, low, rising or falling?”

5

The question is the design brief

Design for the question the reader is likely to ask

A strong visualization anticipates the audience’s main question and makes the answer easy to find. Do not begin with “What can we show from this dataset?” Begin with “What does this audience need to understand?”

What can we show?→What do they need to understand?

Takeaway 2

Purpose comes before design

Audience and purpose are related, but not identical. The same analyst may need one view for monitoring and another for explaining a finding to management.

Why this design? Explain removes branches: one number, one comparison, one sentence.

Takeaway 4

Less can communicate more

Selection is an editorial and analytical act. Simplify the same July 2026 category data and watch the question become easier to answer.

Reader’s questionWhich selected main groups have 12-month price changes clearly above or below overall CPI?

What was removed?

Nothing yet: all 13 main groups, exact values, gridlines and a redundant legend compete for attention.

What was preserved?

The source values and the overall CPI reference at 3.0%.

Statistical integrity

These are group-specific 12-month rates, not contributions to overall CPI.

Selection is part of design

Reduce what the audience does not need

More variables, categories and detail do not automatically create more insight. Removing information can improve understanding when that information is not relevant to the user’s question.
